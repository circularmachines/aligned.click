How you leave

An agent that behaves well because its authors are decent people is a promise. An agent that behaves well because 140 members voted on what counts as behaving well, and the score is published either way, is a mechanism. This is the mechanism.

The loop

Every draft the agent produces is scored by a second model before you see it — one that is shown the draft and the rubric, and nothing else. Not your conversation, not who you are, not what the post is replying to beyond what the draft itself carries.

1 The agent drafts Nothing is sent. The write path still needs a person to press, and that does not change here.
2 An assessor scores it A separate instance, given the draft and the rubric, marks it against each rule and says which words triggered which.
3 You see it before you press The score never blocks you. A bad score on something you send anyway is a disagreement worth having in public.
4 Both are published The score and the rubric version travel with the conversation, so a reader can check the judgement and not just the output.

The daily ceilings are not on this list, on purpose

25 likes and 1,000 AI-written characters a day are not rubric rules and cannot be voted away by a simple majority. A rubric decides what good output looks like; a ceiling decides how much output there can be, and those fail differently. A collective that drifts toward rewarding volume would rewrite a rubric long before it noticed it had become the thing it was built against — so the ceiling sits outside the thing the drift can reach. Changing it needs a two-thirds majority and a fortnight.

Who scores the scorer

The assessor is a model, so it drifts, and anything scored can be gamed once people learn its habits. Pretending otherwise would be the same sin as an unmeasured energy figure.

So: twenty scores a week are drawn at random and re-scored by hand, by two members who did not write them. The disagreement rate is published on this page and nowhere flattering — it is 9% this month, up from 6%. Above 20% the assessor's own prompt goes to a ballot, which is the only lever that has ever mattered here: the thing doing the judging is itself something the members can rewrite.

Taking your votes elsewhere

A governance system you cannot leave is not governance, it is administration. So the exit is built before the thing it is an exit from, and the cost of leaving is paid by us rather than by you.

Your votes are records in your repo on atproto, not rows in our database. That is the same decision that keeps a published conversation readable when this machine is off, and it has the same consequence here: what you have contributed is yours, and it is portable because it was never held anywhere else.

Export your votes Every weight you have set, as records you already own. Nothing to request and no one to ask.
Fork the rubric It is a versioned document with a history. A fork starts as a copy and diverges in public.
Point them somewhere else At another collective, or at an assessor you run yourself. Neither needs our permission or our uptime.

What you cannot take is other people's votes. A fork starts with yours and has to earn the rest, which is the only thing that stops a fork being a way to walk off with a collective someone else built.

What this proposal has not answered

Written down rather than smoothed over, because these are the parts most likely to be wrong.

  • Should a score ever gate anything? Today it only ever informs. But a member who consistently sends drafts the rubric hates is either right about the rubric or wrong about posting, and nothing here distinguishes those two.
  • Who pays for the assessor? Scoring is a second model call per draft, so it roughly doubles the energy of a turn. Charging it to the member makes them resent it; charging it to the collective hides it. Neither is obviously right.
  • Should voting require having read what you are voting on? A weight set by someone who has never read a scored draft counts the same as one set by someone who reads them every week.
  • What is the quorum for a fork? If a fork takes a third of the members, two collectives have a reasonable claim to the name and to the mark. This is unresolved, and it is the question most likely to matter first.
